How ORBIT works

Four steps, one continuous loop — not a one-time assessment.

1

Where the gap is.

ORBIT scans people, pathways, programs, and organizations against the GAPS™ dimensions and flags exactly where readiness is falling short.

→
2

Why it exists.

Every flagged gap is traced to its root cause — a skills shortfall, a broken pathway, a program that isn't converting, an unmanaged risk.

→
3

What to do about it.

Leaders get a specific, prioritized intervention — not a dashboard of red numbers with no next step.

→
4

Whether it worked.

ORBIT tracks the intervention against the original gap and reports the outcome, closing the loop from diagnosis to proof.

This is the loop that separates ORBIT from a reporting dashboard: it doesn't just show you the gap, it verifies the fix. See what that looks like in a sample assessment →

ISO 31000ISO 30414ISO 30401ISO 22301ISO 42001

Step 4's verification is designed in reference to ISO frameworks for risk (31000), human capital reporting (30414), knowledge management (30401), business continuity (22301), and AI management (42001) — alignment, not certification.

From proof to practice

A verified gap isn't the finish line. It's a menu of ways to close it.

ORBIT doesn't stop at step 4 and hand leadership a memo. For every diagnosed gap, it draws from a working library of interventions and builds out the specific combination that fits — not one generic fix stretched across every gap.

Illustrative — one gap, several possible fixes

Which combination gets built depends on what step 2 finds — not a template.

An Advancement gap, for example, could be closed by any of several tools — the mix ORBIT actually builds depends on the root cause, not a one-size answer:

A service inside Workforce Readiness & Risk Governance

ORBIT KCE™ — Enterprise Knowledge Intelligence & Continuity

Most organizations can find where information is stored. Far fewer can answer whether the knowledge that actually runs the business is protected, current, or recoverable. ORBIT KCE™ is built to answer that — the intelligence layer behind the Executive Development and Workforce Readiness & Risk Governance tiers.

Six things it solves for

Knowledge Criticality

Know which knowledge actually matters, and why, across operational, financial, client, and strategic consequence — in one score leadership can act on.

Knowledge Concentration

See past headcount to who actually carries the knowledge. Five people on a process can mean one person effectively holds it.

Successor & Backup Readiness

The difference between "someone else could probably do it" and a validated, capable backup — measured, not assumed.

Tacit Knowledge Exposure

Surfaces the judgment, exceptions, and institutional memory that never made it into a document.

Governance & AI-Readiness

A clear owner, validation date, and access rule for every critical knowledge domain — and a straight answer on whether it's safe for AI to use.

Recovery Readiness

A direct answer to the question that matters: if the person who understands the work became unavailable today, could it continue tomorrow?

This is what turns a Strategic Risk flag into something leadership can actually close — the same loop as every GAPS™ dimension: where the gap is, why it exists, what to do about it, and whether it worked.
